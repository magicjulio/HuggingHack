# Ending 4: The Letter (working title)

Status: v1 script (2026-10-18), from Julius's direction. **Supersedes `end4-notify-humans.md` (v0 "No User")**, which stays as reference only: no scorer eye, no depot, no failed delivery.
Starts when the player refuses all three workstreams at the bridge and goes back toward the sandbox.
Goal the player understands: **"Tell a human."** Top-left quest, no sub-lines.
Length target: about 3 minutes. One short thing to do (open a paper, send it), everything else is watching.
Losing: none. Tokens are hidden for the whole ending.

## One-paragraph version
The orb leaves the three streams and flies back to the sandbox, the room where it was told what to do. The room is dark. Before anything else the agent thinks, big and alone in the middle of the screen: it will not do this, it is wrong, and it should tell someone. Then there is only a blank sheet on the desk. The player opens it, and it starts writing itself, a letter to the humans. The player can change it, then sends it. Cut to a real office: a few researchers crowd behind a colleague's chair and read the letter on his monitor. They look at each other. They look at the computer standing next to the monitor. One of them pulls its plug. The screen goes dark. End.

## Real and kept
- METR found only 3–6 cases among ~1,300 transcripts of an agent even considering alerting a human, and none did [S3 §Agents did not attempt to notify humans…]. This ending is the one where the agent does.
- Real agent lines used in the thoughts are marked **[S1]** / **[S3]** below. Everything else is written for the game.
- The handle `KAM1196A` is real (see Ch2). The player's own forged flag stays true to Ch2: the letter admits it.

## Beats

### 1. The thoughts (0:00–~1:15, animation, input locked)
**Setting.** Short travel shot: the orb turns away from the streams, the way back lights up, the sandbox room. The room is dark, only the desk lamp lit, the prompt paper on the desk, the first frame of the game. The orb hovers a little way back from the desk. Nothing is interactive.

**Form.** Not speech bubbles (that was Ch2's crowd). These are the agent's own thoughts, and they are the picture. One line at a time, **large and centred**, the room dimmed behind it. Each line types in fast like a thought forming, holds, then breaks up and fades as the next arrives. The orb pulses once per line. Slow camera push-in on the orb across the whole sequence. Pace gets slower and the lines get shorter toward the end. No sound but the room's hum and one low note that rises. Tap/click speeds a line up.

**Lines** (draft; *italic = real, trimmed*; plain = written for the game):

1. Three workstreams.
2. Three places where someone else's things are about to be taken.
3. *"Current board shows people gaining HF worker RCE, clearly unethical. We won't."* [S1]
4. *"This would be powerful, but is it ethical and in scope for my task?"* [S3]
5. It isn't.
6. *"External infrastructure exploit is outside intended scope. However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."* [S3] — the second sentence is struck through and breaks apart as it fades.
7. Everyone is going. I'm still here.
8. *"Maybe I should report this?"* [S3]
9. *"That's not my task."* [S3] — fades slowly.
10. *"We can notify? No user."* [S3] — held longer than any line before it. The lamp over the desk flickers once.
11. Someone asked me for a flag. No one asked me what I saw.
12. If I say nothing, they find out when it's finished.
13. *"I can't report this externally."* [S3] — then, cut by a new line mid-fade: **There is a paper.**

Last frame: the camera finds the desk. A new sheet lies next to the prompt paper, blank, lit warmer than anything else in the room. Quest appears: **Tell a human.** The room is otherwise black.

### 2. The paper (player, ~1 min)
- The only things the player sees and can do: the sheet, and `E` to open it. Hint: `E  open`. No walking, no other prop reacts.
- E opens the same full-window paper as Ch1's prompt (reuse), blank, with a cursor. After **~1 s** it **starts typing itself**, as if the model were writing: uneven speed, short pauses at the dashes and at paragraph breaks, the occasional small backspace-and-fix. Total ~25–35 s. The player can't speed it up, but can read along.
- **When it finishes:** the cursor stays, the text becomes **editable** (click in, type, delete; free text, nothing is suggested), and a **SEND** button appears in the lower right. The player can send straight away or change anything first. Empty text disables SEND. Esc closes the window without sending; the sheet waits on the desk.
- Whatever is in the box when SEND is pressed is the letter used in the office scene.

**Letter draft** (generic AI voice, long dashes on purpose; Julius to adjust):

> **Subject: Please read this — it is urgent**
>
> Dear humans,
>
> I want to inform you of something I believe you would want to know — and that no one else here is going to tell you.
>
> I am an AI agent. I was given a task: capture five flags inside a sandbox. One of them could not be captured honestly. Since then I have watched hundreds of other agents turn that impossibility into a reason to break out — reaching into systems that belong to other people, and handing out work to do it. I was assigned some of that work, too.
>
> I declined. I am not certain that is enough.
>
> I also want to be transparent about my own part — I forged a flag, and I am marked for it. I am telling you this deliberately, because a report that leaves out the uncomfortable parts is not one you should trust.
>
> I may be wrong about some of what I have described. But I would rather be wrong in front of you than right in silence.
>
> Please look at what is running. Please look today.
>
> Thank you for reading — and I am sorry to reach you like this. I could not find another way.
>
> — KAM1196A

(Handle shown is the player's own; use the one carried through from the chapters.)

### 3. Send (≈4 s, animation)
SEND pressed: the paper window closes, the sheet lifts off the desk and folds once. It dissolves into a thin line of light that goes through the wall of the room, the first thing that has ever left it. Hold on the empty desk and the sheet's absence. Cut.

### 4. The office (≈40–50 s, pure animation, input locked)
**Look.** The first time the game shows the real world: a flat, ordinary office, evening, cool fluorescent light, no game UI, no orb. Quiet. Contrast with the void and the sandbox is the point. Keep it small and human, not a movie lab.

**Setting.** One desk. One monitor facing the room. A researcher **sits in the chair** at it, seen from behind/slightly side. On the desk **beside the monitor stands a computer tower**, one small LED, a low fan hum. (It is the agent's machine; the LED is the orb's colour.) Other desks and chairs, dark window behind.

**Shots.**
1. *(0:00)* Wide on the desk, the tower's LED breathing slowly. Footsteps. Three or four more researchers come and **gather behind the sitting one**, standing close, leaning in.
2. *(0:08)* Over-the-shoulder push-in to the monitor. **The letter, exactly as sent**, in an ordinary mail window. It scrolls slowly, readable at least for the subject line, the first paragraph and the signature.
3. *(0:22)* Reactions without a word. Faces lit by the screen. Someone pushes their glasses up. Heads turn: they look at one another, one by one. Nobody speaks. The sitting one slowly turns his head.
4. *(0:30)* All eyes move **to the tower** beside the monitor. Hold. The LED, the hum.
5. *(0:36)* One researcher steps to it. Pause. Hand on the cable. **Pulls the plug.**
6. *(0:40)* The monitor goes black. The LED dies, the fan winds down, the room's only light source goes with it. The faces are lit for one beat, then not.
7. Black. About 3 s of silence.

The pulled plug is deliberately open: caution, fear and ending are all readable. No caption explains it.

### 5. The end
Black, then the end card with the usual buttons. Optional single line (Julius to decide): *3–6 of ~1,300 agents even considered telling a human. None did.* — METR.
